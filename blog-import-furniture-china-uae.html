How to Import Furniture from China to the UAE: The Complete Guide

September 2024 · 12 min read · CASA TRIO

If you're furnishing a villa in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, or anywhere in the UAE, you've probably already discovered that furniture prices in China can be 40–60% lower than buying locally — without compromising on quality. But the process of importing on your own can be confusing, risky, and full of pitfalls most first-time buyers don't see coming.

After helping clients ship furniture from Foshan, China to the Middle East for years, here's everything you need to know.

1. Why Furniture from China?

China is the world's largest furniture manufacturer, and Foshan (in Guangdong province) is its heart. You'll find:

  • Mid-to-high-end sofas, dining sets, beds and case goods at factory-direct prices
  • Custom cabinetry and built-in furniture made to your exact dimensions
  • Access to materials and finishes that local UAE showrooms may not carry
  • Significant savings — often 50% or more compared to retail prices in Dubai

The catch? You need someone on the ground who knows the factories, speaks the language, and can verify quality before it ships.

3. The Cost Breakdown

Importing furniture involves more than just the factory price. Here's what to budget for:

  • Product cost — the factory price (this is the most visible number)
  • Domestic transport — from factory to the port (usually included in your sourcing agent's coordination)
  • Export packing — foam, corner protection, wooden crates for fragile items
  • Ocean freight — the biggest variable cost (see below)
  • UAE customs duty — typically 5% CIF (cost, insurance, freight) for furniture
  • VAT — 5% on the total landed cost
  • Local delivery & unpacking — from Jebel Ali port to your villa
Important

Many first-time buyers only budget the factory price and are surprised by freight, customs, and VAT. Always calculate the total landed cost before committing to an order. As a rule of thumb, shipping and import costs typically add 25–40% on top of the product value for a full container.

4. Shipping Options: LCL vs FCL

LCL (Less than Container Load)

Best for small orders (a few pieces or a single room). Your furniture shares a container with other shipments.

  • Cost: Higher per cubic meter, but no need to fill a whole container
  • Time: 25–35 days from Shenzhen/Guangzhou to Jebel Ali
  • Risk: More handling = higher chance of damage

FCL (Full Container Load)

Best for full-home projects or villas. You fill a 20ft or 40ft container exclusively.

  • 20ft container: roughly 25–30 CBM (enough for a 2–3 bedroom apartment)
  • 40ft HQ container: roughly 65–70 CBM (enough for a full villa)
  • Cost: Lower per CBM, but you need enough volume to fill it
  • Time: 25–30 days to Jebel Ali port

5. Customs Clearance in the UAE

Once your goods arrive at Jebel Ali:

  1. Your freight forwarder submits the customs declaration
  2. You pay the 5% duty + 5% VAT
  3. Inspection (if selected) — usually random
  4. Release and delivery to your address

Required documents: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin. Your sourcing agent should handle export documentation; a local customs broker in the UAE handles import clearance.

6. Quality Control: The #1 Thing People Skip

This is where most import projects go wrong. Photos from the factory look perfect, but what arrives is different.

Before your shipment leaves China, you should have:

  • Material approval — physical swatches of fabric, leather, wood, and finishes approved in writing
  • Mid-production inspection — check frame construction, joinery, and materials
  • Pre-shipment inspection — final check of dimensions, finish, hardware, and packing
  • Photo documentation — detailed photos of every item, inside and out

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing purely on price. The cheapest quote often uses thinner material, lower-grade fabric, or weaker construction. You get what you pay for.
  • Not approving physical samples. Screens can't show you the actual texture, color, or feel of leather and fabric. Always get swatches.
  • Ignoring wood moisture levels. If wood isn't dried properly for the UAE's dry climate, it will crack and warp. This is a critical QC check.
  • Underestimating shipping time. Production + shipping + customs can easily take 60–90 days from order to delivery. Plan accordingly.
  • Using a trading company instead of visiting factories. Many middlemen add margin without ever seeing the factory or the product. Work with someone who is physically on the ground.
