How to Source Furniture from Foshan, China: A Practical Guide

September 2026 · 8 min read · CASA TRIO

If you've ever searched for furniture suppliers online, you've probably seen "Foshan" come up. Foshan, in Guangdong Province, is the furniture manufacturing capital of China — and for good reason. It's where a huge share of the world's sofas, beds, dining sets, cabinetry and lighting are produced.

But actually sourcing from Foshan is different from just visiting a showroom. Here's what you need to know before you go.

Where to Go in Foshan

Foshan's furniture industry is concentrated in two main areas:

Shunde District (Longjiang & Lecong)

This is the heart of Foshan furniture. You'll find massive showroom complexes — some multiple stories high — displaying everything from budget to mid-range to high-end pieces. This is where most buyers spend their first few days.

Chancheng District (Shiwan)

This area is more design-focused. You'll find higher-end showrooms, custom furniture studios, and brands targeting the domestic design market. If you're looking for something more unique or design-forward, this is where to spend time.

Practical Tip

Don't try to see everything. A single large showroom complex can take a full day to walk through. Focus on the categories you actually need — don't get distracted by pieces that look great but aren't on your list.

Showrooms vs. Factories

One thing that surprises first-time buyers: most showrooms are not factories. They're display spaces that show products from multiple factories in the region.

This means:

  • The price you see in a showroom includes the showroom's margin, not just the factory price.
  • What's on display may not match the final production quality — samples are often better made than bulk orders.
  • You can't always visit the actual production floor just because you've seen the piece in a showroom.

A good sourcing agent will know which showrooms connect to which factories, and can arrange factory visits when needed.

What to Look For When Visiting

1. Touch everything

Online photos don't tell you about fabric density, leather feel, wood grain depth, or drawer slide smoothness. When you're in a showroom, actually sit on the sofas, open and close the drawers, feel the upholstery. The pieces that feel right in person are the ones that will age well in your client's home.

2. Check construction, not just aesthetics

  • Is the sofa frame solid wood or engineered board?
  • Are the drawer slides soft-close? What brand?
  • Is the jointed furniture dowel-and-screw or just stapled?
  • Can you lift the cushion and see what's inside?

3. Ask about customization

Most factories in Foshan offer some level of customization — fabric choice, dimensions, color, even modifying designs. But the amount of flexibility varies a lot between suppliers. Ask early what can and can't be changed before you fall in love with a piece.

4. Get samples before production

Even if a showroom sample looks perfect, get physical fabric, leather and finish samples approved in writing before production starts. Computer screens and showroom lighting can make colors look very different from what you'll receive.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Choosing based on price alone

The cheapest quote often means thinner fabric, lower-density foam, or hardware from unknown brands. By the time the furniture arrives overseas, the savings are gone in quality issues. Compare total value, not just the unit price.

Mistake 2: Not accounting for all costs

The furniture price is only the beginning. You also need to factor in:

  • Domestic trucking to the port
  • Export packaging (export cartons, foam protection)
  • Ocean freight
  • Insurance
  • Import duties and taxes at destination
  • Last-mile delivery and assembly

These can add 30–60% on top of the product cost depending on your country.

Mistake 3: Skipping pre-shipment inspection

Once a container is loaded and on a ship, fixing problems becomes extremely expensive. A pre-shipment inspection — checking dimensions, materials, workmanship and packaging before the container leaves China — is one of the best investments you can make.
