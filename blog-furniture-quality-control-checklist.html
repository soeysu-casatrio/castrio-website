Furniture Quality Control Checklist for Overseas Buyers

September 2026 · 10 min read · CASA TRIO

The #1 risk when importing furniture from China is not price — it's receiving something different from what you approved. A sofa that looks perfect on a factory photo can arrive with the wrong fabric, weak joints, or a finish that doesn't match your samples.

After inspecting hundreds of orders before shipment, here's the QC checklist we use on every project. If you're sourcing furniture yourself, this is what you need to verify before the container leaves China.

Why QC Matters

Once furniture is on a ship, fixing a problem becomes expensive and slow. You can't easily return a sofa from Dubai to Foshan. The time to catch issues is before packing, before loading, before shipping.

Rule of Thumb

A $500 inspection catch in the factory saves you a $5,000 problem once it's on the water — and a damaged reputation with your client.

1. Before Production: Material Approval

QC doesn't start when the furniture is finished. It starts when materials are chosen.

Material Approval Checklist

Fabric/leather swatches approved in writing. Don't rely on photos — get physical swatches and sign off on color, texture and grade.
Wood type confirmed. Is it solid oak, walnut veneer, or MDF with a laminate? Know exactly what you're buying.
Finish sample approved. Matte, satin, gloss? What color? Get a physical finish sample.
Hardware brand confirmed. Blum, Hettich, DTC? Don't assume it's the premium brand.

2. Mid-Production Check

About halfway through production, check the frame and construction before upholstery or finish is applied.

Construction QC Checklist

Frame material. Hardwood, pine, or engineered wood? High-use pieces should have solid hardwood frames.
Joinery. Dovetail drawers should actually have dovetails. Check corner blocks on sofas.
Wood moisture content. Critical for dry climates like the Middle East. Wood that's too wet will crack and warp.
Spring system. Serpentine springs vs. pocket coils? Ask what's inside — don't assume.

3. Pre-Shipment Final Inspection

This is the most important check. Once production is complete and before packing, inspect every item.

Final Inspection Checklist

Dimensions. Measure width, depth, height. Do they match the approved drawings?
Color match. Compare against approved swatches under natural light, not just factory lighting.
Upholstery. Check for wrinkles, misaligned patterns, loose stitching, and fabric pulls.
Finish quality. Look for drips, orange peel, uneven sheen, or sanding marks.
Hardware function. Open and close every drawer, door, hinge. Do they work smoothly?
Structural test. Sit on sofas, open and close tables, check for wobble or creaking.
Packing. Corner protectors, foam wrap, and wooden crates for fragile items. Is it container-ready?

5. How to Document QC

Every inspection should be documented with:

  • Overall photos of each piece from all angles
  • Close-up photos of materials, stitching, hardware
  • Measurement photos with a tape measure visible
  • Packing photos before container loading
  • A written report listing any issues and their resolution

Should You Inspect Yourself or Hire Someone?

If you're based overseas, you have three options:

  1. Fly to China yourself — expensive, but you see everything firsthand
  2. Hire a third-party QC company — they inspect but don't know your products or standards
  3. Work with a sourcing partner who does QC — someone who knows your project, your standards, and the factories

The most common mistake is trusting the factory's own QC. Factories inspect their own work the way students grade their own homework.
